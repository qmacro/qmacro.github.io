DJ Adams has been writing this blog since 2002, chronicling a journey through technology that stretches from early advocacy of REST and HTTP as a proper application protocol, through the SAP Fiori and UI5 era, into functional programming, Unix philosophy, shell scripting, and the architecture of SAP's Cloud Application Programming Model (CAP). What distinguishes the blog is its author's dual identity: a deeply embedded SAP ecosystem veteran -- community co-creator, Developer Advocate, O'Reilly author -- who continually draws on the wider traditions of Unix, the open web, and computing history to illuminate the enterprise technology he works with daily. The voice is that of a perpetual learner, warm and personal, as comfortable discussing the Acorn Atom that sparked a lifelong fascination with computing as it is dissecting hexagonal architecture or the mechanics of CDS bindings. Technical posts favour hands-on demonstration over abstraction -- curl commands, REPL sessions, worked examples -- and are often grounded by personal texture: the coffee being drunk, the narrowboat being lived on, the 1984 paper that changed how a script was written. Across a quarter century the recurring thread is a conviction that timeless principles -- simplicity, composability, proper use of protocols, agnostic design -- matter more than any particular technology, and that the best way to understand them is to get your hands dirty and share what you find.
